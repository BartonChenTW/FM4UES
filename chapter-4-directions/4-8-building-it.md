## 4.8.1 Data generation and sampling design

Since ground truth comes from a simulator, **the training distribution is a design decision, not a given**. This is the most under-appreciated step.

### What to sample

- **Configurations** — device types, capacities, storage sizes, topologies (Tier 2+)
- **Boundary conditions** — weather years, demand profiles, prices, carbon intensity
- **Operating regimes** — including rare ones

### Sampling methods

The sampling plan is a design decision the building-surrogate literature already records: a review of 57 building-design [surrogate](../appendices/a-glossary.html#surrogate) studies tabulates each one's sampling strategy alongside its objective and surrogate type.[^westermann2019surrogate]

| Method | Use |
| :--- | :--- |
| Latin Hypercube[^mckay1979lhs] | good default coverage of continuous design space |
| Sobol / quasi-random[^sobol1967distribution] | better uniformity in high dimensions |
| Adaptive / active learning | concentrate samples where surrogate error is high |
| Stratified by regime | guarantee coverage of rare states |

### The rare-regime trap

{: .important }
**Take this seriously.** A model learns the distribution it is trained on, so it is least reliable where its training data is thinnest, and in energy systems those are often the events that matter most.

In energy systems the rare regimes are the ones that matter most: cold snaps driving peak heat demand, Dunkelflaute, storage fully depleted, network constraints binding. Uniform sampling of the design space will under-represent all of them. **Stratify deliberately.** Learning continuous targets where some values have far fewer observations is a recognised machine-learning problem in its own right — deep imbalanced regression — with dedicated methods.[^yang2021dir]

### Normalisation across carriers

Carriers differ by orders of magnitude in typical values: a household's electricity demand is in kilowatts, a district heating plant's output in megawatts. Per-carrier standardisation is the minimum; consider physical non-dimensionalisation (fractions of capacity, fractions of peak demand), which additionally helps transfer across systems of different sizes.

### Synthetic and archetype-derived data — what it buys and what it does not

See [§3.2](../chapter-3-sim-opt/3-2-building-simulation-data.html) for this in full — the summary: simulator-generated corpora are fully labelled, but archetype-derived data supports interpolation across archetype space, not generalisation to real heterogeneity, and the corpus silently absorbs the generating optimiser's assumptions.

### Data that cannot leave its owner

A corpus generated by a simulator from archetypes, weather and schedules contains no one's meter readings, so a model trained on it has no household to memorise. That is these notes' own reasoning; no study was found that tests it. Real operating data comes back in three places: fine-tuning a pretrained model to real buildings, validating it against real operation ([§5.5](../chapter-5-case-study/5-5-risks.html), risk 4), and the metadata-conditioned load model of [§4.6](4-6-reading-the-screen.html#most-promising-near-term-limited-by-access-to-data). Household meter data is personal data. European data-protection authorities treat it as such, partly because a load curve can single out a household even without an account identifier.[^art29wp2011smartmetering] Pseudonymised data that can be attributed to a person with additional information is still personal data.[^eu2016gdpr] [§2.8](../chapter-2-fm-foundations/2-8-ues-fm-evaluation-criteria.html) (dimension 8) gives the evidence on what meter data reveals.

**Four ways to train without pooling the raw data.** None is free.

| Route | What leaves the data owner | Formal guarantee | What the evidence shows |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **[Federated learning](../appendices/a-glossary.html#federated-learning)** | Model updates, not data | None by itself | On 100 London households, federated training was less accurate than pooling the data; only federated training followed by personalisation to each home beat models trained on each home alone, by about 5%.[^briggs2022federated] Another study on the same data found federation 20–40% less accurate than pooling.[^delgadofernandez2022federated] For heat pumps, federated learning alone converged poorly with little data, and domain-informed data augmentation closed the gap.[^balint2023tclfl] A curious server reconstructed clients' training windows from shared updates on London and Portuguese meter data.[^meijer2025tsinverse] |
| **[Differential privacy](../appendices/a-glossary.html#differential-privacy)** | Model updates or outputs with calibrated noise | Yes, with a privacy budget ε (smaller is stronger) | On 100 London households, MAPE rose from 8.4% to 10.6% at ε = 4.2.[^delgadofernandez2022federated] On 50 BDG2 buildings, differentially private federated training had 17–37% lower error than non-private training at ε from 0.5 to 6.5; its authors attribute this to gradient clipping and flag it as a single-seed result confounded by the architecture that differential privacy required.[^ameh2026dpfl] On aggregated load, the cost differed widely between forecasting methods.[^eibl2018dpload] |
| **Synthetic data from a generator trained on meters** | A generator, or its samples | Only if the generator was trained with differential privacy[^huang2023dpwgan] | The generator is itself a model trained on personal data. Training profiles can reappear in a GAN's output without any attack.[^huang2022ganleakage] On London meter data, a standard membership test found nothing, yet only ε = 1 stopped deliberately inserted unusual households from being reconstructed; ε = 8 barely helped (preprint).[^chai2024defininggood] |
| **Data from a simulator** | Nothing personal | Not needed | Avoids memorising households, but inherits the representativeness limits of archetypes ([§3.2](../chapter-3-sim-opt/3-2-building-simulation-data.html)). If the simulator was calibrated on measured data, personal data enters at that step only |

**Foundation models can be trained this way.** A time-series foundation model has been pretrained from scratch with each of 18 public datasets as a separate client,[^chen2025ffts] and, in a simulation with 174 clients drawn from one large public corpus, more data, more clients and higher participation each improved the federated model.[^chen2026fedal] Adapting an existing model is cheaper: sharing only small adapter weights cut communication 86-fold in one study, where plain fine-tuning on small clients fell below [zero-shot](../appendices/a-glossary.html#zero-shot) accuracy, and the best configuration, with differential privacy at ε = 5 per round, cut error by 31% against zero-shot (preprint, on commodity prices).[^sharma2026fedchronos] On energy data, personalised federated adaptation of three time-series foundation models across 50 BDG2 buildings improved on global federated training and matched or beat purely local training (preprint).[^nihalchandani2026fedtsfm] All of these split public data into simulated clients; no federation across organisations that actually hold separate data was found, and none tests whether the federated model leaks.

**The trained model is not anonymous by default.** The European Data Protection Board's position is that a model trained on personal data is anonymous only if both the likelihood of extracting personal data from it and of obtaining such data through queries are insignificant, and it lists tests against [membership inference](../appendices/a-glossary.html#membership-inference), attribute inference, model inversion and reconstruction.[^edpb2024aimodels] Such attacks work on smaller load models. Whether a customer's data was used to train a deep load forecaster could be detected well above chance (preprint);[^johansson2025tsmia] query access alone to a personalised load forecaster revealed properties of the consumer almost as well as access to the meter data itself;[^aly2026propertyinference] and when an imputation model pretrained on public data was fine-tuned on meter data treated as private (London households, ASHRAE buildings), membership of the fine-tuning data was detectable with AUROC up to 0.90, where 0.5 is chance (preprint).[^taleb2026lbrm] That last setting, pretrain on public data and fine-tune on private data, is how an energy foundation model would be adapted. No such test of an energy foundation model was found, which is [gap G11](../chapter-6-outlook/6-1-open-gaps.html#g11).

**Access is governed by law, not licences.** The open corpora these notes rely on allow training and sharing models, with attribution: Low Carbon London,[^ukpn2022lcl] IDEAL,[^goddard2021idealdata] the [BuildingsBench](../appendices/d-model-index.html#buildingsbench) data[^emami2023buildingsbenchdata] and NREL's end-use load profiles[^wilson2021eulpdata] are all CC BY 4.0. BDG2's repository has carried different licence texts over time, so cite the archived version 1.0, which is CC BY 4.0.[^miller2020bdg2zenodo] A Creative Commons licence settles copyright and database rights, never data protection, and on Creative Commons' own conservative reading a model trained on ShareAlike data should be released under the same licence.[^cc2025aitraining] The open residential sets are small: IDEAL, the one that pairs meter data with household survey and building attributes, has 255 homes.[^pullinger2021ideal] The much larger utility-held data is restricted by law:

- **EU.** Research reuse of personal data is compatible with its original purpose under safeguards, and must use the least identifying form that serves the research.[^eu2016gdpr] Each household has the right to its own meter data and to pass it to a party of its choice.[^eu2019electricitydirective] The access procedure for third parties is per customer and per energy service;[^eu2023meteringdata] on these notes' reading, a research team assembling a training corpus is not covered by it. Since September 2025, even non-personal data from connected devices such as heat pumps and inverters may be passed to third parties only to fulfil the manufacturer's contract with the user.[^eu2023dataact]
- **Switzerland.** Network operators may process pseudonymised 15-minute load curves only for listed grid purposes, and research is not among them. Since 1 January 2026, cantonal universities, the ETHs, ETH-domain research institutes and universities of applied sciences can request anonymised metering and master data for research from the national data platform.[^ch2008stromvv] Whether the platform already serves such requests was not checked.

**What this means for building one** (these notes' own reading). Pretrain on simulated data and openly licensed corpora. Fine-tune on private data where it is held, locally or by federated training with personalisation, and add differential privacy if the adapted model will be shared. Before releasing weights fine-tuned on household data, run the attacks the EDPB lists. For a Swiss research team, the research route in Swiss electricity law is the first door to try.

## 4.8.2 Enforcing physics

Four mechanisms, in increasing order of strength and cost:

| Mechanism | How | Guarantee | Cost |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Soft penalty** | add constraint violation to loss | none | free |
| **Architectural** | design outputs so constraints hold by construction (bounded decoders, softmax splits) | exact for what is encoded | low |
| **Projection / repair** | post-process onto feasible set | exact | moderate |
| **Exact encoding in solver** | encode NN as [MILP](../appendices/a-glossary.html#milp) constraints[^fischetti2018milp] | exact + optimality | high |

Notes from practice:

- Bounded decoders and physics-informed regularisation preserve operational feasibility in GNN-based OPF, but residual violations may still require post-processing for strict feasibility.[^wen2026lghgnn]
- Physics-informed approaches embedding multi-physics dynamic constraints in the loss enable physically consistent solutions with limited training samples and improve accuracy under sparse data — a valuable property when simulator runs are expensive.[^karniadakis2021piml]
- Feasibility-restoration layers are an active area; repair layers for networks with hard constraints are being developed as reusable components.[^chu2026snarenet]

**Recommendation for multi-carrier energy systems:** architectural enforcement of per-carrier energy balance (make the outputs sum correctly by construction), plus soft penalties for inequality constraints, plus a projection step if hard feasibility is required downstream. See also [§5.4](../chapter-5-case-study/5-4-data-modules-and-tasks.html#physics-loss) for the physics-loss inventory attached to these notes' specific case-study representation.

## 4.8.3 Evaluation protocol

A credible protocol reports all six. Accuracy alone does not show whether a model's output can be used.

1. **Accuracy** — error on held-out instances, per carrier, per horizon
2. **Feasibility** — constraint violation rate and magnitude; energy balance residual
3. **Optimality gap** (Tier 3) — cost versus true optimum
4. **Speedup** — honestly reported, **including** data generation and any post-processing correction. Reporting both can change the picture by an order of magnitude: one power-flow model reports up to 190× speedup over a conventional solver before its outputs are corrected for feasibility, and over 10× after.[^wen2026lghgnn]
5. **Transfer** — performance on unseen configurations/topologies; this is the actual foundation-model claim
6. **Calibration** — if probabilistic, are the intervals honest?

{: .note }
Where the domain community has an established accuracy measure, use it alongside generic ML metrics. For building energy, calibration guidelines such as ASHRAE Guideline 14, IPMVP and FEMP set the accuracy measures practitioners already read;[^ruiz2017calibration] [CV(RMSE)](../appendices/a-glossary.html#cv-rmse), used in the worked example of [§4.1](4-1-off-the-shelf-fms.html), is the most familiar. Reporting them costs nothing and makes results easier for that audience to judge.

### Held-out design

Random splits overstate performance. Hold out along the axis you claim to generalise over:

- unseen **configurations** (Tier 1/2 claim)
- unseen **topologies** (Tier 2 claim)
- unseen **archetypes / typologies** (building-scale claim)
- unseen **weather years / climate regimes**
- unseen **scales** (train small, test large)

A model trained and tested at one scale and horizon has not shown that it works at others. If transfer across scales is part of the claim, test it explicitly.

## 4.8.4 Realistic budget expectations

For a small project, such as one research group without a dedicated compute cluster, these notes' rough guide is:

| Feasible | Not feasible |
| :--- | :--- |
| [Fine-tuning](../appendices/a-glossary.html#fine-tuning) existing models of tens to hundreds of millions of parameters | Pretraining a large model from scratch |
| Thousands to hundreds of thousands of simulator runs for training data | Tens of millions of runs |
| Small scaling studies | Large architecture sweeps |
| Releasing a benchmark and dataset | Sustained large-scale compute |

{: .important }
**At this scale, the realistic deliverable is a representation, a dataset, a benchmark and a demonstrated small model, not a large trained [foundation model](../appendices/a-glossary.html#foundation-model).** A claim to *establish what a foundation model for this domain requires, and demonstrate feasibility* can be backed by that. A claim to *build a foundation model* cannot.
